# Fixed Income and Guaranteed-Return Options in India

Rates in this file are typical ranges as of 2025-26 and move over time. Always
confirm the current rate with a web search before quoting an exact number.

## Savings Account
Interest is roughly 2.5-4% at large banks and 6-7% at some small finance banks.
Fully liquid. Interest up to Rs 10,000 per year is deductible under Section 80TTA
(Rs 50,000 under 80TTB for senior citizens). Deposits are insured up to Rs 5 lakh
per bank per depositor by DICGC. Use it only for the money you need this month.

## Fixed Deposit (FD)
You lock a lump sum for a fixed tenure at a fixed rate. Typical rates are 6.5-7.5%
at large banks and 7.5-8.5% at small finance banks, with an extra 0.25-0.75% for
senior citizens. Tenure ranges from 7 days to 10 years.

Key points:
- Interest is taxed at your income slab rate every year on accrual. For someone in
  the 30% slab a 7% FD is about 4.9% after tax, which can be below inflation.
- Breaking early usually costs a 0.5-1% penalty on the applicable rate.
- TDS is deducted once interest crosses Rs 40,000 per year (Rs 50,000 for seniors).
  Submit Form 15G/15H if your total income is below the taxable limit.
- A 5-year tax-saving FD qualifies under Section 80C but is locked for 5 years.
- Laddering (splitting money across several FDs of different tenures) keeps some
  money liquid and reduces reinvestment risk.

Best for: money needed in 1-3 years, emergency reserve, and low risk tolerance.

## Recurring Deposit (RD)
You deposit a fixed amount every month for a fixed tenure, usually 6 months to 10
years, at roughly FD rates. Interest is taxed the same way as FD.

Best for: building a lump sum from monthly salary when you cannot take equity risk
- for example a planned expense 12-24 months away. For horizons over 5 years, a
SIP in a mutual fund has historically produced more, at the cost of volatility.

## Public Provident Fund (PPF)
Government backed, 15-year tenure, extendable in 5-year blocks. Rate is set
quarterly, historically around 7.1%. Minimum Rs 500 and maximum Rs 1.5 lakh per
financial year.

Key points:
- EEE tax status: contribution qualifies under 80C, interest is tax free, and
  maturity is tax free. This makes the effective return much better than an FD at
  the same headline rate for anyone in a high tax bracket.
- Partial withdrawal is allowed from year 7, and a loan from years 3 to 6.
- Interest is calculated on the lowest balance between the 5th and last day of the
  month, so deposit before the 5th.

Best for: long-horizon, zero-risk debt allocation and 80C usage.

## Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) and VPF
Salaried employees contribute 12% of basic pay, matched by the employer. The rate
is around 8.25%, the highest guaranteed rate available in India. Voluntary
Provident Fund (VPF) lets you contribute more at the same rate.

Note: interest on employee contributions above Rs 2.5 lakh per year is taxable.
Withdrawal is tax free after 5 years of continuous service.

Best for: retirement. VPF is usually a better deal than an FD for salaried people.

## National Pension System (NPS)
Market-linked retirement account with equity, corporate bond and government bond
options. Very low expense ratio. Deduction of up to Rs 1.5 lakh under 80C plus an
extra Rs 50,000 under 80CCD(1B), which is over and above the 80C limit.

Trade-off: locked until age 60, and at maturity at least 40% must be used to buy
an annuity, which is taxed as income. Best used for the extra Rs 50,000 deduction
rather than as your only retirement vehicle.

## Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)
For a girl child under 10. Rate is around 8.2%, EEE tax status, Rs 250 to Rs 1.5
lakh per year, matures at 21 years or on marriage after 18. The best guaranteed
long-term product available if you are eligible.

## Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS)
Age 60+, rate around 8.2%, 5-year tenure extendable by 3, limit Rs 30 lakh,
quarterly payouts. Qualifies under 80C. Interest is taxable.

## Government and Corporate Bonds
G-Secs and state development loans can be bought through the RBI Retail Direct
platform with no credit risk. Corporate bonds and NCDs pay 1-3% more but carry
issuer default risk - check the credit rating and never chase a high-yield NCD
from a weak issuer.

## Which one to pick
- Money needed within 12 months: savings account, liquid fund or short FD.
- 1-3 years: FD, RD, or a short-duration debt fund.
- 5+ years and you are risk averse: PPF, VPF, plus NPS for the extra deduction.
- High tax bracket: prefer PPF, EPF and debt funds over FDs, because FD interest
  is taxed every year at slab rate.
