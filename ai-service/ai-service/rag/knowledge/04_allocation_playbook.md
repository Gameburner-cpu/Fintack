# Asset Allocation Playbook - Matching Money to Time Horizon

The single most useful question when someone asks "where should I invest?" is
**when do you need this money back?** Risk capacity comes from time, not from
personality.

## The order of operations

1. **Emergency fund**: 6 months of essential expenses in a savings account or
   liquid fund. Non-negotiable, comes before any investing.
2. **Insurance**: term life cover if anyone depends on your income, plus health
   cover.
3. **Clear high-interest debt**: paying off a credit card at 36-42% annualised, or
   a personal loan at 12-18%, is a guaranteed, tax-free return that beats any
   investment. Do this before investing.
4. **Then invest**, matched to horizon.

## Horizon buckets

### Under 1 year
Savings account, liquid fund, overnight fund, or an FD matching the date.
Zero equity. Expected 4-7%. Capital preservation is the only objective.

### 1 to 3 years
FD, RD, short-duration debt funds, arbitrage funds. Up to about 10-20% in a
balanced advantage fund if the person is comfortable with small swings.
Expected 6-8%.

### 3 to 5 years
Roughly 40-60% equity through hybrid or large-cap index funds, the rest in debt.
Expected 8-11% with visible year-to-year variation.

### 5 to 10 years
Roughly 60-75% equity, mostly index and flexi cap funds via SIP, with a small
mid-cap slice; the rest in PPF, EPF or debt funds. Expected 10-12%.

### 10+ years
75-85% equity, including a mid and small cap allocation, plus PPF/EPF/NPS for
the debt portion and 5-10% gold. This is the only horizon where equity's
volatility reliably works in your favour.

## Rules of thumb for the split
- A common starting point for equity share is (100 minus age)%, adjusted up or
  down for how much volatility the person can actually tolerate without selling.
- Rebalance once a year back to the target split. This mechanically sells what
  has run up and buys what has lagged.
- Never let a single stock exceed about 5-10% of the portfolio.

## Sizing by amount

- **Under Rs 50,000**: emergency fund first. If that already exists, one index
  fund SIP is enough - do not spread a small amount across five funds.
- **Rs 50,000 to Rs 5 lakh**: two or three funds maximum. An index fund, a
  flexi cap and a debt fund covers everything most people need.
- **Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 lakh**: add tax planning (ELSS or NPS), a gold allocation,
  and consider staggering entry through an STP rather than a single lump sum.
- **Above Rs 25 lakh**: worth paying a SEBI-registered fee-only adviser once for
  a plan. Estate planning and a nomination review start to matter.

## Market conditions
Timing the market reliably is not achievable for retail investors, but valuation
does affect the sensible approach:
- **Expensive market** (high Nifty PE versus history): keep SIPs running, stagger
  any lump sum over 6-12 months, avoid concentrated small-cap bets.
- **Cheap market after a fall**: increase SIP amounts if you can, deploy lump sums
  faster. Falls are when long-horizon investors get their best entry prices.
- **Rising interest rates**: short-duration debt funds and fresh FDs benefit;
  long-duration gilt funds lose value in the short term.
- **Falling interest rates**: lock into long-tenure FDs before rates drop further;
  long-duration debt funds gain.
Whatever the conditions, the SIP should not be stopped. Stopping SIPs during
falls is the single most common and most costly retail mistake.

## Worked example
Rs 10,000 a month, 30 years old, 10-year horizon, moderate risk, emergency fund
already in place:
- Rs 6,000 into a Nifty 50 index fund SIP
- Rs 2,000 into a flexi cap fund SIP
- Rs 1,500 into PPF (tax-free debt, 80C)
- Rs 500 into a gold ETF
Review once a year, increase the SIP by 10% each year with salary growth. At 11%
average, this reaches roughly Rs 23-26 lakh in 10 years; with the annual step-up,
noticeably more. These are historical averages, not guarantees.
