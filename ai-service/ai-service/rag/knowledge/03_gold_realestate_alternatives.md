# Gold, Real Estate and Alternative Assets

## Gold

Ways to hold it:
- **Physical jewellery**: making charges of 8-25% are lost immediately, plus 3%
  GST, purity risk and storage cost. The worst way to hold gold as an investment,
  though fine as a consumption purchase.
- **Coins and bars**: 3% GST, lower making charge, still storage and resale
  friction.
- **Gold ETFs**: bought through a demat account, tracks the gold price, expense
  ratio around 0.5%, easy to sell. Practical default for most investors.
- **Gold mutual funds**: fund-of-fund route, SIP possible, no demat needed,
  slightly higher cost than an ETF.
- **Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB)**: paid 2.5% annual interest on top of the gold
  price and capital gains were tax free if held to maturity at 8 years. Note that
  new SGB issuance has been discontinued - existing bonds still trade on the
  exchange, sometimes at a premium. Check current availability before recommending.
- **Digital gold**: convenient but sits outside SEBI/RBI regulation. Prefer ETFs.

Role in a portfolio: gold is a hedge against currency weakness and crises, not a
compounding asset - it produces no income. A 5-10% allocation is a common
recommendation. Indian retail gold prices sit roughly 15-18% above the
international spot price because of import duty and GST, and jewellery adds
making charges on top of that.

## Real Estate

Buying property:
- Realistic total cost is 8-12% above the sticker price: stamp duty 5-7%, GST on
  under-construction property, registration, brokerage, legal fees, interiors.
- Rental yield in most Indian cities is only 2-4% of property value per year,
  which is below an FD. The return case rests on capital appreciation and on the
  fact that a home loan enforces disciplined saving.
- Home loan tax benefits: up to Rs 2 lakh of interest under Section 24(b) and
  principal under 80C for a self-occupied property.
- Illiquid. Selling can take months and costs 1-2% in brokerage.
- Highly concentrated - one property often becomes 70-80% of a household's net
  worth, in a single location.

Rules of thumb:
- Do not buy a house unless you expect to stay 7+ years.
- Total EMI across all loans should stay under about 40% of take-home pay.
- Down payment plus registration should not wipe out the emergency fund.
- Renting is not "wasting money" when rent is 2-3% of property value while a home
  loan costs 8-9% - the maths often favours renting and investing the difference.

**REITs** (Embassy, Mindspace, Brookfield, Nexus and others) let you own
commercial real estate from a few hundred rupees, listed and liquid, distributing
most of their income. Yields have generally been in the 5-7% range. A far more
practical way to add real estate exposure to a small portfolio than buying a flat.

**InvITs** are the same idea for infrastructure assets such as roads and
transmission lines.

## Other Options

- **Crypto**: extremely volatile, taxed at a flat 30% on gains with no loss
  set-off, plus 1% TDS on transfers. Treat as speculation. If someone insists,
  a very small allocation of money they can afford to lose entirely.
- **P2P lending**: 10-14% advertised, but defaults are real and RBI has tightened
  the rules. High risk despite the "fixed return" framing.
- **Corporate FDs and NBFC deposits**: 1-2% above bank FDs, with genuine default
  risk. Check the credit rating.
- **Endowment and money-back insurance policies**: typically return 4-5% and mix
  insurance with investment badly. Keep the two separate - buy pure term
  insurance for protection and invest the rest.
- **ULIPs**: better than they used to be but still opaque with a 5-year lock-in.
  An index fund plus term insurance is usually cheaper and clearer.

## Insurance is not investment
Term life cover of roughly 10-15x annual income, plus health insurance of at least
Rs 5-10 lakh, should be in place before any serious investing. A single
hospitalisation without cover can undo years of SIPs.
