# Mutual Funds, Stocks and Market-Linked Investing in India

## Mutual Fund Categories

### Equity funds
- **Large cap**: top 100 companies. Lower volatility, historically 10-12% over
  long periods. Core holding for most people.
- **Mid cap**: companies 101-250. Higher growth and much higher drawdowns.
- **Small cap**: 251 onwards. Can fall 50-60% in a bad year. Only for 7+ year
  horizons and only as a small slice.
- **Flexi cap**: the fund manager moves across market caps. Good single-fund choice.
- **Index funds and ETFs**: track Nifty 50, Nifty Next 50 or Sensex passively.
  Expense ratio 0.1-0.3% versus 1-2% for active funds. Most active large-cap funds
  fail to beat their index after fees over 10 years, so index funds are the
  sensible default for the equity portion of a portfolio.
- **ELSS**: equity fund with an 80C deduction and a 3-year lock-in, the shortest
  lock-in of any 80C option.

### Debt funds
- **Liquid / overnight**: park money for days or weeks, very low risk, redeem in
  one working day.
- **Ultra short and low duration**: 3-12 month money.
- **Short duration and corporate bond**: 1-3 years.
- **Gilt funds**: government securities, no credit risk but sensitive to interest
  rate moves.
Debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rate regardless of holding
period, which removed their old indexation advantage over FDs. They are still
useful for liquidity and for avoiding annual TDS.

### Hybrid funds
- **Aggressive hybrid**: 65-80% equity, taxed as equity. Good first fund.
- **Balanced advantage / dynamic asset allocation**: shifts equity exposure with
  valuations, smoother ride.
- **Arbitrage funds**: near debt-like returns but taxed as equity - useful for
  parking money for 3-12 months in a high tax bracket.

## SIP versus Lump Sum
A SIP invests a fixed amount monthly, averaging your purchase price and removing
the need to time the market. For a lump sum in equity, an STP (park in a liquid
fund and transfer weekly or monthly into equity over 3-6 months) reduces the risk
of buying everything at a peak.

SIP maths: Rs 10,000/month for 10 years at 12% grows to roughly Rs 23 lakh on
Rs 12 lakh invested. The same SIP for 20 years reaches roughly Rs 92 lakh. Time
matters far more than the amount - starting five years earlier usually beats
increasing the SIP by 50%.

## Direct versus Regular Plans
Direct plans have no distributor commission, typically 0.5-1% lower expense ratio.
Over 20 years that difference alone can be worth 15-20% of the final corpus.
Always choose Direct if you are picking funds yourself.

## Direct Stocks
Buying individual shares requires a demat account and real research. Realistic
guidance:
- Keep direct stocks to a minority of the equity allocation until you have several
  years of experience.
- Diversify across at least 15-20 names and multiple sectors, or you are taking
  concentration risk you are not being paid for.
- Never invest borrowed money or your emergency fund in stocks.
- Ignore tips, Telegram groups and "multibagger" claims.

## Futures and Options (F&O)
SEBI's own studies have repeatedly found that the large majority of individual
F&O traders lose money - roughly 9 out of 10 in the equity derivatives segment -
with average losses per loss-making trader running into lakhs of rupees.

F&O is leveraged speculation, not investing. It is appropriate only for hedging an
existing position, or for professionals with a tested edge and strict risk limits.
For someone asking "where should I invest my savings", F&O should be excluded, and
the reason stated plainly. If a user insists, the honest guidance is: never risk
more than a tiny share of net worth, understand that options can expire worthless,
and note that leverage magnifies losses as much as gains.

## Taxation of Market Investments (post-July 2024 rules)
- **Equity and equity mutual funds**: short term (held under 12 months) taxed at
  20%. Long term (12 months or more) taxed at 12.5% on gains above Rs 1.25 lakh
  per year.
- **Debt mutual funds** bought after 1 April 2023: taxed at your slab rate.
- **Dividends**: taxed at slab rate in the investor's hands.
- **Tax harvesting**: booking up to Rs 1.25 lakh of long-term equity gains each
  year and reinvesting resets your cost base tax free.
Confirm current thresholds with a web search - they change with each budget.

## Costs to watch
Expense ratio, exit load (often 1% if you redeem an equity fund within a year),
brokerage, STT, and the bid-ask spread on thinly traded ETFs. Small percentages
compound into large amounts over decades.
