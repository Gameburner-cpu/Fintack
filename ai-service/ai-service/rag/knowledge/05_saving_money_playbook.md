# Money-Saving Playbook - Concrete Tactics with Rupee Impact

Use this with the user's real transaction data. Every suggestion should carry a
number computed from their actual spending, not a generic tip.

## Subscriptions

- Annual plans are typically 15-25% cheaper than paying monthly. A Rs 199/month
  service costs Rs 2,388 a year; the annual plan at around Rs 1,499 saves roughly
  Rs 890. Only switch for services used for 6+ months already.
- Audit every recurring charge quarterly. Unused subscriptions are the most common
  silent leak - people routinely find Rs 500-1,500 a month of forgotten charges.
- Family or duo plans split across 4-6 people cut per-person cost by 60-75%.
- Cancel and resubscribe seasonally rather than paying year-round for something
  used in bursts.

## Credit cards used correctly

A credit card is a saving tool only if the full statement balance is paid every
month. Revolving credit costs 36-42% annualised, which erases any reward.

Category strategy:
- **Fuel**: fuel co-branded cards waive the 1% fuel surcharge and add 4-5% back.
  On Rs 4,000/month of fuel that is roughly Rs 2,400-2,900 a year.
- **Groceries and online shopping**: cashback cards give 5% on select merchants.
  On Rs 8,000/month that is around Rs 4,800 a year.
- **Dining and delivery**: many cards give 10-20% off through partner programmes.
- **Travel**: travel cards earn points worth 2-4% when redeemed for flights, plus
  lounge access worth Rs 3,000-8,000 a year for frequent flyers.
- **Utilities and rent**: usually excluded from rewards, and rent payment apps
  charge 1-2%, which cancels the benefit. Pay these by UPI.

Rules: keep total cards to 2-3, set autopay for the full amount, never withdraw
cash on a credit card (interest starts immediately at 2.5-3.5% a month), and match
the annual fee against actual usage - a Rs 5,000 fee card is only worth it if the
benefits actually get used.

## Food and delivery
Delivery costs 40-60% more than the same food cooked at home, once delivery fees,
platform fees, packaging and surge pricing are added. Cutting delivery from 12 to
4 orders a month at an average of Rs 350 saves around Rs 2,800 a month, about
Rs 33,600 a year. Meal planning and one weekly grocery run reduce both spend and
waste.

## Transport
- Compare monthly cab spend against a public transport pass; in most metros the
  pass wins decisively.
- Fuel efficiency: correct tyre pressure and steady driving improve mileage
  5-10%.
- If a personal vehicle is used less than around 8-10 times a month, cabs are
  usually cheaper once EMI, insurance, servicing and parking are counted.

## Bills and utilities
- Compare mobile and broadband plans yearly - long-tenure recharges are 10-20%
  cheaper per month than monthly ones.
- Electricity: five-star appliances, and AC at 24-26°C rather than 18-20°C, cuts
  consumption noticeably.
- Set up autopay to avoid late fees, which are pure loss.

## Debt
Always attack the highest interest rate first: credit card revolving balance,
then personal loan, then car loan, then education loan, then home loan. Paying
off a Rs 50,000 card balance at 40% saves Rs 20,000 a year - no investment
matches that with certainty.

Prepaying a home loan early in the tenure saves a large amount of interest, but
weigh it against the 80C and Section 24(b) tax benefits and against the return
available on an equity SIP over the same period.

## Behavioural tactics that actually work
- **Pay yourself first**: automate the SIP or transfer for the day after salary
  credit, so saving is not left to willpower at month end.
- **The 24-hour rule**: for any non-essential purchase over Rs 5,000, wait a day.
  A large share of intended purchases evaporate.
- **Cost per use**: Rs 8,000 shoes worn 200 times cost Rs 40 a use; Rs 2,000 shoes
  worn 10 times cost Rs 200 a use. Cheap is not always cheaper.
- **Annualise small leaks**: Rs 150 a day is Rs 54,750 a year. Framing daily spend
  as an annual figure changes decisions.
- **Step-up saving**: increase the SIP by 10% each year with salary growth. This
  raises the savings rate without ever feeling like a cut.

## Priority order for suggestions
When advising a specific user, rank by rupees saved per year:
1. High-interest debt interest
2. Unused or badly structured recurring charges
3. The single largest discretionary category in their data
4. Card and payment-method optimisation on their biggest categories
5. Small habit changes
Always show the annual rupee figure. "You could save about Rs 18,000 a year" is
actionable; "try to spend less on food" is not.
